👁️What it reads, and where

The short version: karma-giving watches every channel; AI chat, GIF reactions, and web search are confined to two channels; nothing else gets read.

Feature

💬Chatting with the bot

@mention it in an active channel

Tag @KNYWBot with your question or your nonsense in #knywbot-testing or #💀-town-square and it replies — casual, clan-voice, not a customer-service bot. Mentioning it anywhere else does nothing.

It can actually search the web now

For anything current or specific — build guides, patch notes, real links — it can run a real search and cite the actual page it found, instead of guessing. It's told to stick to what the search results actually say rather than filling gaps from memory, but it's still a small free model: treat confident-sounding specifics (exact item/skill names) with a healthy amount of skepticism, especially for very recent content.

Defaults to Diablo 4, unless you say otherwise

Ambiguous questions get answered as Diablo 4 by default (it won't blend in Path of Exile/WoW/etc. terminology). Name a different game explicitly and it'll answer about that game normally instead — no refusing, other games are fine to talk about.

It knows today's actual date

So "what's next season" or "how old is this patch" get answered against the real calendar instead of a guess from training data.

It can post a boost or group request for you, mid-conversation

Ask it directly — @KNYWBot I need a boost to lvl 70 or @KNYWBot find me a group for helltides — and it'll post the request itself, same as running the slash command. For boosts you can also ask on someone else's behalf by @mentioning them: @KNYWBot hey, @someone needs a boost to lvl 70 posts it under their name, not yours. See Boost requests and Group requests for the full flow.

It remembers your conversation — briefly

Follow-ups keep context for up to the last 10 messages between you and it. Go quiet for 60 min and it forgets — next mention starts fresh. This memory lives only in the bot's running memory (RAM); a bot restart clears everyone's context instantly, and it's never written to disk.

Anti-flood limits

Per person: 1 msg / 12s cooldown, and 50 messages/day. Server-wide: at most 3 replies being generated at once — if you're rate-limited you'll get a short heads-up instead of silence (except when the server-wide limit is the cause, which stays silent to avoid adding more noise while already busy).

Steered away from a few topics

It's instructed to decline or steer off real-money-trading/gold-selling/account-selling talk and cheat/exploit/botting discussion. Standard AI safety filtering (harassment, hate speech, explicit content) applies underneath regardless. Beyond that it's told to be permissive — normal game banter, trading talk, tier-list complaints etc. are never refused.

Feature

🎬GIF reactions

It's watching for a good moment — not every message

In the same two channels as chat, every message (not just @mentions) is a candidate. On each one, it rolls a 15% chance to even consider reacting, and won't consider another one within 240s of its last GIF — so it can react at most once every 4 minutes, and usually less often than that.

When it does consider one, an AI model judges the vibe

It's instructed to be picky — funny, hype, a brag, a fail, a rage-quit moment — and to skip anything ordinary. It reads the last few messages too, so it can catch a punchline that only makes sense with the build-up, not just the latest line in isolation. If it decides something fits, it posts a matching GIF as a reply.

Feature

⭐Karma

Only MODZ can give or take karma. A regular member typing @user++ or @user-- does nothing — it's silently ignored. The one exception is the automatic award for completing a boost request (below), which any booster can earn regardless of role.

Give or take karma with ++ / -- MODZ only

A MODZ member types @user++ (or --) anywhere in a message, in any channel — no command needed. The bot reacts with ⬆️ to confirm it registered. Can't be used on yourself or on bots, and there's a 5s cooldown per person so it can't be spammed.

/karma and /karma-leaderboard

/karma [user] checks anyone's total (defaults to yourself) — open to everyone. /karma-leaderboard shows the top 10 in the server.

/karma-request — ask a MODZ member to award you karma

Posts a public Approve / Deny prompt to the MODZ member you name — only they can click it, since only MODZ can actually give karma. Naming someone without the role gets rejected up front instead of posting a prompt nobody can approve. Requesting from yourself, from a bot, or too frequently (30s cooldown) isn't allowed.

Earned automatically by boosting not MODZ-gated

Completing a boost request (see below) awards the booster +1 karma automatically, regardless of role — this is the one way a regular member's karma changes without a MODZ member doing it by hand.

Feature

🚀Boost requests

A help-request system for #looking-for-boost — ask for a carry, a Booster claims it, you coordinate in a dedicated thread, and the channel stays clean once it's resolved.

Two ways to ask

/request-boost opens a short form (just "what do you need help with?"). Or just ask the bot directly in chat — @KNYWBot I need a boost to lvl 70 — and it posts the same thing for you. You can even ask on someone else's behalf by @mentioning them in the message.

What gets posted

An embed in #looking-for-boost pinging the Booster role, with Claim and Cancel buttons, plus a dedicated thread for coordination — no DMs needed.

  1. Claim — any Booster can claim it (not their own request). It's assigned exclusively; the channel post updates to show who claimed it, and they're added to the thread.
  2. Coordinate — all further conversation happens in the thread, not the main channel.
  3. Complete — both sides must agree — a "Mark Complete" button appears in the thread. Both the requester and the booster have to click it before it actually closes out; one confirmation just shows "waiting on the other person."
  4. Once both confirm: the booster gets +1 karma automatically, and the whole post + thread is deleted from the channel — nothing stale lingers.

Cancel (before claim) & Repost (if a booster backs out)

Before anyone claims it, the requester (or a mod) can hit Cancel — the post is deleted outright. If a booster claims it and then flakes, the requester can click "Booster backed out — repost" on the claimed post: the old post is deleted and a fresh listing goes up with the same description, no retyping needed.

Claim, cancel, complete, and repost all log to #mod-logs — who did what, and how much karma changed hands.
Anyone mentioned in a listing shows their BattleTag automatically, if they've set one.

Feature

🧭Group requests

Same idea as boost requests, for group activities instead of a single exclusive claim — #looking-for-group, no role pinged, up to 3 others can join.

Two ways to ask

/request-group opens a short form ("what are you looking to do?"). Or ask the bot directly — @KNYWBot find me a group for helltides — and it posts the same thing for you.

What gets posted

An embed in #looking-for-group — no role ping this time — with Join and Disband buttons, plus a dedicated thread for coordination.

  1. Join — up to 3 other members can join (not the leader themselves, no duplicates). The post updates live to show who's in and how many slots are left.
  2. Once full (3/3), the Join button disappears — only Disband remains.
  3. Disband — the leader or a mod can call it off at any point, whether it's empty or full. The post and its thread are both deleted, same clean-channel approach as boost requests.
Unlike boost requests, there's no completion step or karma reward here — once the group's formed, you just go do the thing. Disband is how you close it out when you're done.

Feature

🏷️BattleTags

Self-reported, not scraped — Discord doesn't let bots read a linked Battle.net connection even if it's set to show publicly on your profile, so this is opt-in instead.

/battletag-set

Set your own BattleTag (e.g. Name#1234). MODZ can set it for someone else too, by adding the user option — handy for setting one up on someone's behalf.

Or just tell the bot

@KNYWBot set my battletag to Name#1234 works too — no command needed. This chat method only ever sets your own tag, though: even a MODZ member asking this way can't set it for someone else — that still requires /battletag-set with the user option.

/battletag (private) vs /bt (public)

/battletag [user] checks anyone's tag, visible only to you. /bt posts your own tag publicly in the channel.

A boost/group listing shows whatever BattleTag was on file at the moment that message was posted or last updated — it's not live. If you change your tag, older still-open listings won't automatically pick up the new one.

Feature

📅Events

A real Discord Scheduled Event (shows in the server's Events tab, native RSVP) plus a custom reminder poster in #events, since Discord's own event reminder only fires once, right at start time, with no lead time.

/event-create MODZ only

Title, description, a date (YYYY-MM-DD) and time (24h HH:mm, interpreted in the server's configured timezone), plus optional duration, location, and a repeat schedule (weekly / biweekly / monthly). Creates the Discord event and immediately posts an announcement to #events.

/event-list

Open to everyone. Shows every upcoming event with its time and a live "interested" count pulled straight from Discord.

/calendar

Open to everyone. Posts a visual month-view calendar right in the channel — a text grid marking which days have something on, plus a list of that month's events underneath. Prev ◀ / Next ▶ buttons let anyone page through other months without re-running the command. This is a read-only mirror of the same calendar a mod sees in the admin panel — creating/editing/cancelling still goes through /event-create, the admin panel, or /event-cancel.

/event-cancel MODZ only

Takes the event ID shown in /event-list. Deletes the Discord event and stops any further reminders for it.

Reminders

A background check posts to #events at configurable lead times before start — by default 24h, 1h, and 15min — pinging whoever holds the Event Ping role. That role is meant to be opt-in — ask a MODZ member how to grab it (typically set up as a reaction role).

A recurring event automatically creates its next occurrence once the current one has passed, with reminders reset for that new date.
MODZ also get a full interactive calendar in the admin panel — click any day to create an event there, click an existing one to edit or delete it. It's the exact same events /calendar and /event-list show; the panel is just where the create/edit/delete side lives instead of the slash commands.

Feature

🎭Roles

Join role

New members automatically get a Member role the moment they join — nothing to do on your end.

Booster role

Whoever holds the Booster role can claim requests in #looking-for-boost. Fully self-service — just ask the bot directly: @KNYWBot give me the Booster role and it's granted on the spot, no approval needed. Taking a break? @KNYWBot pause my booster status removes it temporarily, and @KNYWBot unpause me restores it later. You can also ask on someone else's behalf by @mentioning them — anyone can do this for anyone, not just MODZ.

Reaction roles 1 active

React with a specific emoji on a message a mod has wired up, and you instantly get a matching role; remove your reaction to remove the role. Currently set up in #react-welcome — react ✅ there to grab the Member role.

Event Ping role

Opt-in — holding it means you get pinged when an event is announced or about to start. Not tied to any special access, purely a notification preference.

Restricted

🔧MODZ commands

Gated by the MODZ role specifically — not Discord's built-in permissions. Someone can have full server admin rights and still not be able to run these without the role, and vice versa. The bot has no kick/ban/timeout/purge commands of its own — that stays in Discord's native moderation tools, used by hand.

/warn

Note a tracked warning against a member — DMs them and logs it. This is a record-keeping tool, not an automatic punishment; nothing happens to the member's access.

/warnings

View a member's warning history and count.

/clearwarnings

Reset a member's warning history.

/karma-set

Manually correct a member's karma total.

/battletag-set (for others)

Set a BattleTag on someone else's behalf.

/event-create

Schedule a clan event with reminders — see Events.

/event-cancel

Cancel a scheduled event and stop its reminders.

/reactionrole-add

Point it at a message ID, an emoji, and a role — it reacts to the message with that emoji and registers the mapping in one step, so members can immediately start self-assigning.
